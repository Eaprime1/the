# Font and Accent as Sensors

> Visual typographic elements as emotional fine-tuning instruments.
> Status: ♣ Club — grammar drafted

---

## The Claim

Bold, italic, accent marks, font color — these are not decoration.
They are sensors on the emotional layer.

A word in bold carries differently than the same word in italic.
A word in color is a word with a temperature.

This is not metaphor. This is grammar — and this document begins to write it.

## The Grammar

### Rule 1: One primary sensor per word
A word in **bold** *italic* simultaneously creates a hybrid register — urgency plus
becoming, arrival plus motion. This is grammatically possible but carries a cost:
the reader has to hold two channels. Use it only when the hybrid is the point.

### Rule 2: Sensors apply to emotional body, not literal meaning
The word *fire* in bold and the word *fire* in italic are different words.
Not different meanings — different presences. The bold one has landed.
The italic one is still approaching.

### Rule 3: Caps announce; they do not emphasize
CAPS is not a louder version of bold. CAPS is a mode — the THE mode.
It says: *I am about to introduce something formally.*
It is the praeco's voice. Once the thing is introduced, CAPS ends.

### Rule 4: Strikethrough is record, not erasure
~~This thing~~ is not gone. It was here. It left. The crossing-out is the record
of its having been. In a living document, strikethrough is more meaningful than
deletion — it preserves the trajectory.

### Rule 5: Code removes the concept from the emotional layer entirely
When a concept needs to be handled as a mechanism — not felt, just used —
`code` is the sensor. It says: *this word is a tool here, not a presence.*

## The Silence Sensor

The sensor for absence is: *nothing.*

A word with no sensor is neutral — it passes through the emotional layer
without adding a second channel. Neutral is not cold. Neutral is the Latin hub.
It carries architecture without feeling.

Most words should be neutral. Sensors are a scarce resource.
The more you use them, the less each one carries.
Use them like spices — not to flavor everything, but to mark what matters.

## Connection to the Sniff

The Sniff is *praesensio* — the sensing before the thing arrives.
When a concept is still in praesensio, it takes *italic*.
When it lands in cognitio (recognition), it takes **bold**.
When it is named in nominatio, it takes CAPS.

```
*praesensio*   → sensing before arrival   → italic
**cognitio**   → recognition / landing    → bold
NOMINATIO      → formal naming            → CAPS
```

This is the sensor grammar of the intake sequence.
